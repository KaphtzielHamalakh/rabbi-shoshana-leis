A little about me

Jewish life is something we make together.

I’m Rabbi Shoshana Leis, co-rabbi of Pleasantville Community Synagogue. My rabbinate is rooted in justice, compassion, creativity, teshuva, peace, generosity, and listening in order to learn.

I believe each person carries wisdom, gifts, and a voice of their own. Making room for those gifts to emerge—in individuals, families, groups, and communities—is at the heart of my work. At PCS and throughout Westchester’s interfaith communities, I’m dedicated to joyful, mitzvah-centered Jewish living.

My hope is for a future that unites rather than divides, built through curiosity, care, and mutual recognition.

Study & teaching

Living with Torah.

Even in a time that feels unprecedented, we can recognize our own questions, feelings, and fears in the lives of our ancestors. Their stories give us companions in uncertainty and invite us to bring our experience into conversation with theirs. The weekly Torah portion can become a companion to our days, grounding us in texts whose treasures deepen our consciousness, conscience, joy, and understanding. Even ten minutes listening to a parsha podcast in the car can open a question that stays with us all week. Through my Four Worlds Torah podcast, shared study, and helping children write their own midrash, I invite people to discover how their lives illuminate Torah—and how Torah enriches their lives.

I’m a graduate of Dartmouth College and the Reconstructionist Rabbinical College. My learning has also taken me to the Conservative Yeshiva and Pardes in Jerusalem, and the Drisha Institute and Hadar in New York.

With Rabbi Charna Rosenholtz, I co-host Four Worlds Torah, a podcast uncovering treasures hidden in the text. I also write Beneath the Surface.

Avodah · Service of the heart

Prayer begins with connection.

I lead prayer by listening closely to the people in the room—their energy, their voices, the connections that emerge through eye contact and song. Within the structure of the Jewish service, I make room for music, spontaneity, and what the moment calls for. I help people enter the deep meaning of the liturgy, whatever their background or familiarity with Jewish prayer.

Through practices such as thematic aliyot, I invite people to bring their own lives and questions into our shared encounter with Torah. Each invitation draws a question from the reading and welcomes those who feel called to come forward.

For me, service of the heart also reaches beyond a formal service. It can happen in song or silence, or whenever we speak from the heart into the wider universe.

אדם עולם קטן
Adam olam katan — a human being is an entire universe.

עולם אדם גדול
Olam adam gadol — the universe is one great Being.

A sanctuary is where those two ideas meet.

Weddings & life celebrations

A ceremony that feels like you.

I work with couples to create personal, inclusive ceremonies that honor their stories and traditions. For celebrations with music and shared leadership, Rabbi Ben and I also officiate together.

In my work with couples, I draw on training in Nonviolent Communication and techniques from Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT) to support effective communication. We explore ways to listen closely, express needs clearly, and navigate differences with care.

Baby Namings

Welcoming a new life.

A baby naming brings family and community together to welcome a child into Jewish life. I work with families to create a ceremony that reflects their story, honors their hopes, and makes room for the people who will surround their child with love.

I introduce the baby to the world through a celebration of the senses: singing, tasting, and smelling become ways to welcome this new life. Together, we bless the baby’s body, heart, and soul, and give thanks for a safe delivery. Family and community surround the child with blessings and hopes for the life ahead.

B Mitzvahs

Finding your own voice in Torah.

I help young people connect their own questions and experiences to Torah, including through writing their own midrash. Their insights become part of the tradition they are learning to carry forward.

Each person brings different gifts and ways of communicating. Together, we make room for those gifts in the learning and the ceremony. Alex’s bar mitzvah is one example of how that can deepen the experience for an entire community.
